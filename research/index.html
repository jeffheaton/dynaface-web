Facial Paralysis and Synkinesis Research

Dynaface has been used in peer-reviewed studies by researchers at Johns Hopkins to measure facial movement from video in idiopathic facial paralysis (Bell's palsy), oral-ocular synkinesis, and facioscapulohumeral muscular dystrophy (FSHD). The researchers behind these studies are listed on the Contributors page, and every measurement named below is defined on the Measurements page.

Published Studies

AI facial metrics and patient-reported outcomes in idiopathic facial paralysis

Dynaface measured the Facial Asymmetry Index (FAI), palpebral fissure, and oral commissure excursion from video of patients with idiopathic facial paralysis at rest and during smile, laughter, and pucker. Greater asymmetry while smiling and laughing was associated with worse FACE-Q scores for appearance, facial function, and self-esteem, while more symmetric commissure movement during a smile was associated with better scores.

Renne, A., Heaton, J., & Boahene, K. D. O. (2026). Associations of AI-based facial metrics with patient-reported outcomes in idiopathic facial paralysis. Laryngoscope, 136(7), 2991–3001. https://doi.org/10.1002/lary.70417

Quantifying oral-ocular synkinesis

A case-control study of 30 patients with synkinesis at least 12 months after idiopathic facial paralysis and 12 controls, measuring the palpebral fissure at rest and during smile, laugh, and pucker. Puckering produced the largest change: the eye on the paralyzed side narrowed by 30% (22% on the other side), compared with under 2% in controls.

Renne, A., Heaton, J., Derakhshan, A., Nellis, J. C., Desai, S. C., & Boahene, K. D. O. (2026). Use of dynamic, automated facial analysis in quantifying oral-ocular synkinesis. Facial Plastic Surgery & Aesthetic Medicine, 28(3), 305–314. https://doi.org/10.1177/26893614251395737

In an invited commentary on this study, Wamkpah and Pepper of Stanford, who are not affiliated with Dynaface, noted that it tracks facial movement over time instead of relying on static images, which can miss dynamic movement and invite image selection bias, and that it is free to the public. They also cautioned that the clinical significance of palpebral fissure differences has yet to be defined.

Wamkpah, N. S., & Pepper, J.-P. (2026). Invited commentary on “Use of dynamic, automated facial analysis in quantifying oral-ocular synkinesis,” by Renne et al. Facial Plastic Surgery & Aesthetic Medicine, 28(3), 315–316. https://doi.org/10.1177/26893614251404008

Facial weakness in facioscapulohumeral muscular dystrophy

A cross-sectional study of adults with FSHD that combined FACE-Q responses from 191 participants with Dynaface analysis of a subset. The objective measurements showed reduced eye closure and smile movement, and abnormal dental display and commissure excursion were associated with worse FACE-Q scores.

Berges, A. J., Renne, A., Heaton, J., Leung, D. G., & Boahene, K. D. O. (2026). Facial weakness in facioscapulohumeral muscular dystrophy: Objective and patient-reported measures to guide reconstructive interventions. Facial Plastic Surgery & Aesthetic Medicine. Advance online publication. https://doi.org/10.1177/26893614251407675

Using Dynaface in a Study

  • Recording. Have the subject face the camera directly, centered in the frame, with even front lighting, a steady camera, and the same distance from session to session. Short video clips of each expression (the studies above used rest, smile, laugh, and pucker) are easier to analyze than one long recording. For profile photos, take a true side view with the head level.
  • Video. Dynaface opens MP4, MOV, and M4V files and analyzes up to 5,000 frames per video, skipping any frame where no face is found. Trim long recordings to the clips you need first.
  • Millimeters. Measurements are converted to millimeters using an assumed pupillary distance of 63 mm. Enter each subject's measured pupillary distance in Settings when absolute sizes matter; ratios and angles are unaffected.
  • Export. Measurements export to CSV, with one row per frame and a time column for video. Annotated images, and annotated MP4 video on Mac, Windows, iPhone, and iPad, can be saved for figures and review. For batch processing, the Python library computes the same measurements from code.
  • Data handling. All analysis runs on the device; no images, video, or measurements are uploaded, and no account is needed. IRB approval, HIPAA, and other institutional requirements remain the responsibility of the researcher's institution. See the FAQ and Legal page for details.
